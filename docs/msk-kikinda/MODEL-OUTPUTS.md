**Three conclusions the founder must internalise:**

1. An **IRR-ratchet call gets more expensive every year** (106 → 127 between Y8 and Y12) while a **MOIC call gets cheaper every year** (59 → 48), because interim cash received reduces the MOIC top-up but the IRR clock keeps compounding. These move in opposite directions. The founder wants MOIC; the investor wants IRR.
2. A pure IRR-ratchet call is priced at a **60–75% premium to the fair value of what is being bought**, and is **not fundable** by refinancing at 3.5x EBITDA at any point in the model. It is not a founder protection — it is an investor protection the founder has been persuaded to want.
3. The only designs that are simultaneously fundable and accretive are **MOIC-capped calls exercised late (Y10+)**. They deliver the investor ~10.5–11.5% IRR at a 2.0–2.25x multiple.

**No combination clears all three tests in the base case.** The call option cannot be both cheap enough to be worth exercising and rich enough to satisfy a 12% investor hurdle, because the asset does not grow fast enough to outrun the investor's required return. Mechanisms 1–4 cost zero. **The overrun waterfall is worth more negotiating effort than the headline split.**
