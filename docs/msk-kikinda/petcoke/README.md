## The price to buy against

**Argus fob US Gulf 6.5% S / 40 HGI** is the seaborne benchmark for US Gulf and
midcontinent coke, published weekly. Recent levels: **Q3 2025 fob offers
USD 67–77/t; Q4 2025 6.5% S non-calcined USD 54.90/t**, down 16.2% on the quarter.

Argus's own outlook is that higher heavy sour crude runs on the US Gulf may lift
high-sulphur fuel-grade output in 2026 and **pressure fob 6.5% prices** — the
supply side is working for a buyer.

*A widely-quoted "USD 478/t FOB US petcoke" figure is **calcined** coke — a
different product, for anodes. Do not use it.*

## The discount nobody else can take — USD 11/t

The benchmark is set by cement and power buyers, who are penalised for sulphur
and need grindable coke. **MSK is neither.** A gasifier with AGR + Claus/TGTU is
indifferent to sulphur — it recovers it and sells it — and a slurry mill does not
care about HGI the way a cement kiln does.

| | USD/t |
|---|---|
| sulphur 7–8% instead of 6.5% | −4 |
| HGI 30–35 instead of 40 | −4 |
| high V/Ni | −3 |

**MSK should never pay the benchmark.** Worth ~€2.4m/y. (V/Ni also raise slag and
refractory questions — that is the trade, and it needs the licensor's view.)

## Port of Bar takes a Supramax — just

**Max draft 12.5 m, up to 80,000 DWT, LOA 260 m, 2,700 m of berth.** A 58,000 dwt
Supramax draws ~12.8 m at full summer deadweight, so expect to lift **~53,000 t**
rather than a full cargo. At 241 kt/y that is **4.5 cargoes a year**.

## Freight dominates — so the Mediterranean has to be tested

Ocean freight plus rail is **59% of the delivered cost**. The cargo itself is a
minority of the bill.

| Route | FOB USD | Freight USD | Delivered €/t |
|---|---|---|---|
| US Gulf (Houston) | 58 | 41 | **127.8** |
| Mediterranean refinery | 80 | 14 | **123.2** |

**Mediterranean petcoke wins up to FOB USD 85/t — a USD 27/t premium over US
Gulf** — because it saves USD 27/t of freight. US Gulf FOB is the cheapest in the
world and may still not be the cheapest *delivered*. It also cuts transit from
~28 days to ~5, roughly halving the €12.5m of working capital tied up in cargo.

**Tender both.** Spain, Italy, Greece and Turkey all have coking refineries.

## Freight evidence

June 2026, 50,000 t petcoke Houston → Iskenderun, spot laycan: **~USD 41.50/t**
(April 2026: ~USD 35/t). Same week Houston → ARA: ~USD 33.50/t. Bar is ~600 nm
shorter than Iskenderun from Gibraltar but is a thinner market with poorer
backhaul, so owners will not pass the full saving on — hence USD 36–47/t.

## Correction to Part 6

Part 6 assumed ocean freight of **USD 26/t** on the base case. The observed
market is **USD 41/t**. Part 6's delivered base of €115.8/t should be
**€127.8/t**, or €117.7/t with the grade discount. At 241 kt/y that is
**€2.9m/y** worse than Part 6 carried, before the discount claws it back.
